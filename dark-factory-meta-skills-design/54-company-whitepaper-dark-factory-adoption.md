# Software-Assured Dark Factory Adoption Whitepaper

## Executive Thesis

Software-Assured Dark Factory, or SADF, is a governed agentic delivery model for organizations that want the speed of autonomous coding agents without losing the trust, auditability, and handoff rigor expected from professional outsourcing firms.

The goal is not to remove humans from accountability. The goal is to move humans to the highest-value control points: business intent, approval boundaries, risk acceptance, production ownership, taste, and learning. Agents perform the repetitive SDLC labor under a strict control plane: interrogation, standards tailoring, task ledgers, artifact generation, implementation, verification, expert critique, traceability, release handoff, and continuous improvement.

## Why This Exists

Traditional outsourcing works because it gives the client a disciplined relationship:

- scope baseline;
- checkpoints;
- project plans;
- requirements and design artifacts;
- test reports;
- traceability;
- handoff packages;
- change management;
- production support documentation.

Pure agentic coding often fails because it skips that relationship. A dark factory that cannot prove what it did, why it did it, how it was tested, who approved it, and what remains risky will not earn serious enterprise trust.

SADF combines both worlds:

- professional SDLC rigor from ISO/IEEE, RUP, MDA, DDD, TDD, BDD, SRE, and secure SDLC;
- dark-factory execution through agents, scenarios, holdouts, browser checks, sandbox runs, and convergence loops;
- client-style governance through approvals, token budget SWAGs, stage gates, change control, and handoff evidence.

## Operating Model

SADF has three layers.

| Layer | Purpose | Accountability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta-meta attractor | Converts broad, evolving, contradictory intent into a stable product-tailored factory run | Engagement owner and Hawkeye auditor |
| Meta-skill factory | Instantiates the right child skills, roles, artifact set, workflow, tests, and gates for the project | Governance mayor and delivery owner |
| Execution factory | Produces artifacts, code, tests, evidence, reviews, dashboards, handoffs, and learning records | Work package owners and quality refinery |

The meta-meta layer prevents overfitting to one example product. A todo/habits app can test the machinery, but a healthcare product, fintech workflow, devtools API, internal enterprise platform, or production migration must receive a different generated meta-skill contract.

## Trust Controls

SADF earns trust through visible controls.

| Trust Concern | SADF Control |
| --- | --- |
| "The agent skipped steps." | Runtime scheduler, task beads, PERT dependencies, Hawkeye audit, and next-action preflight |
| "The spec was ambiguous." | Customer interrogation rounds, answer IDs, contradiction scoring, completeness scores, and approval mechanics |
| "The documentation is fake." | Template-as-proof rejection, physical evidence records, trace links, reviewer records, and quality certificates |
| "The tests are gamed." | Scenario matrix, holdout tests, transfer tests, browser evidence, digital-twin-style sandboxes where applicable |
| "The result is not maintainable." | HLD/LLD, ADRs, DDD maps, runbooks, onboarding portal, training records, and maintenance handoff |
| "The model forgot context." | Project book, knowledge graph, context packs, predecessor recovery, replay drills, and dashboard index |
| "The cost is uncontrolled." | Token SWAGs, checkpoint approvals, reapproval triggers, and change requests |

## Lifecycle

SADF follows a full lifecycle rather than a single prompt-to-code jump:

1. Meta-attractor gate.
2. Engagement governance and token budget.
3. Customer interrogation and recursive spec decomposition.
4. Standards tailoring and artifact BOM selection.
5. Feasibility, risk, and architecture alternatives.
6. Implementation planning and PERT/work-ledger setup.
7. Construction through small approved work packages.
8. Verification: unit, integration, contract, scenario, holdout, transfer, browser, accessibility, security, performance, and production checks as applicable.
9. Quality refinery: specialist critics, adversarial reviewers, RALPH loops, scorecards, and certificates.
10. Transition: release notes, deployment guide, rollback plan, runbook, outage drill, and human training.
11. Maintenance and learning: brownfield impact analysis, regression evidence, retrospectives, and skill updates.

## Human Collaboration

Humans remain part of the factory:

- They approve scope, budget, risk, production, legal, security, and irreversible decisions.
- They can take over any node.
- Agents can resume human work after a structured handoff.
- Async reviews are classified, linked, incorporated, and reverified.
- Disagreements are recorded as alternatives, recommendations, owner decisions, and residual risks.
- Operators learn through outage drills and runbook replay, not by being handed a mysterious codebase.

## Difference From A Normal Consulting Delivery

| Dimension | Traditional Outsourcing | SADF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labor model | Human teams perform most work | Agent swarm performs most work under human governance |
| Budget | Dollars and calendar | Tokens, compute, model class, and human approval time |
| Artifacts | Human-authored handoff docs | Generated and reviewed project-book artifacts with executable evidence |
| Verification | QA teams and manual reviews | Scenario harnesses, tests, browser checks, sandboxes, critics, and judges |
| Traceability | Often document-centric | Graph and ledger-centric with forward and reverse links |
| Change control | PM and client board | Governance mayor, token SWAG, impact closure, human approval |

## Adoption Path

| Phase | Recommended Scope | Exit Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 | Artifact-only and review-only factory runs | Project-book and traceability evidence pass |
| Phase 2 | Bounded greenfield apps with UI and tests | Browser, scenario, and handoff evidence pass |
| Phase 3 | Brownfield changes in low-risk repos | Regression, impact, rollback, and review gates pass |
| Phase 4 | Internal production services | SRE handoff, observability, incident drill, and owner signoff pass |
| Phase 5 | High-risk/regulated systems | External audit, legal/security approval, and domain-specific certification plan |

## Non-Negotiable Boundary

SADF must not claim literal infallibility. It must claim only what evidence proves. "Zero mistakes" becomes:

- zero tolerance for unsupported claims;
- zero acceptance of template-as-proof;
- zero hidden skipped stages;
- zero unowned residual risk;
- zero production claims without production evidence.

## Decision

SADF is ready as a governed methodology and local control-plane prototype. It is not yet an enterprise-hosted autonomous delivery platform until the always-on scheduler, project-specific runtime adapters, source-refresh automation, and production-scale simulation are executed and certified.
